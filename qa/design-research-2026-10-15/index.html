Study 00 / Overview

Tempo: four families, different jobs

Read source report ↗

Research and first implementation pass · 15 October 2026 · candidate branch codex/tempo-calm-ui.

Design decision

Keep one familiar navigation system, but make the work area match the job. Different background colors alone do not solve the repetition. Lists should read like lists, nutrition like a diary, routines like a sequence, and thoughts like notes.

PlannerWarm paper #faf8f2Ruled To-do sheet; aligned Calendar time rows; illustrated goal cardsBlue calendar structure, orange actions, sage goals
Daily rhythmHoney cream #fff8ecMorning/evening columns; next step before checklist; immersive wake controlsAmber daytime, muted violet evening, green completed steps

Brain dumpPale violet #f6f3fcCapture first, then paper notes; searchable saved factsViolet structure, warm paper content, concise type labels

Today keeps the approved warm timeline. Ask Tempo remains directly accessible, with conversation taking priority over suggestions. Main groups retain the existing hover/click/touch/keyboard flyouts.

Evidence, without overclaiming

Eight reports cover Today/To-do; Calendar/Goals/Reminders; Routines/Wake; Nutrition; Meals/Recipes/Groceries; Fitness; Brain dump/Assistant; and Onboarding/Settings. Each includes source links, screenshots, limitations, implementation paths and acceptance criteria.

The strongest direct competitor interaction was Eat This Much's anonymous generator, which returned a sample meal plan. Muse's public entry/download chooser was exercised. MyFitnessPal, Motion, Tiimo and Todoist authenticated workflows were not available. Their official guides and published product screens support visual decisions, not claims about tested persistence. Paprika and Hevy were studied through official product/help illustrations. No competitor account was created or modified.

The user could not sign in to MyFitnessPal and requested public research instead. Its official Today guide and the user's supplied screenshot anchor the nutrition direction. Tempo uses its own components, identity and existing assets; competitor screenshots remain research evidence, not production assets.

What the Sol implementation team changed

  • Planner: quieter To-do sheet; Calendar date strip and time/source alignment. Existing illustrated Goals retained.
  • Rhythm/Brain: guided Morning/Evening routine cards, next-step action, paper Thoughts, clearer Memories, readable conversation.
  • Food: Nutrition Tracker naming, week picker, optional calorie target and remaining value, colored macro cards, meal diary groups; searchable Recipes; separate shopping/pantry; selected workout before templates.
  • Shared shell: four family foundations and related subtab treatments. Stable section IDs and account data contracts remain unchanged.

This is an initial visual implementation with local checks. It is not a completed release or a new live-integration certification. Preview interactions reset on reload and do not establish account persistence.

Next implementation briefs

1. Validate the tracker on a real isolated account

Read report 04 and NutritionView.tsx, nutrition-model.ts, FoodEntryEditor.tsx, NutritionTargetEditor.tsx. Retain nutrition as the deep-link ID. Exercise search/manual/barcode fallbacks, portions, edit/copy/delete, selected dates, optional targets, partial data and reload. Assert actual stored nutrients and ownership; never infer success from the progress bar. Do not invent intake or default calorie targets. Run nearest tests, lint/typecheck and affected browser journeys.

2. Finish each family using its own report

Read reports 01–03 and 05–08; assign one owner per component and one shared-shell integrator. Improve empty/loading/error states using real data, not decorative charts. Validate desktop, 390px mobile, keyboard navigation, long text and reduced motion. An author must not be the sole reviewer.

3. Complete the release gates separately

Use the existing release checkpoint and live-journey records. Re-test phone sign-in and number correction, same-account SMS/dashboard updates, Google Calendar confirmation, reminder delivery, restart/retry behavior, and isolation. Study 01 / Planner

Today and To-do — visual research, 2026-10-15

Read source report ↗

Evidence and access

This is a competitor visual study, not an interactive app audit. Sources were opened in the Codex in-app browser on 2026-10-15. Motion, Tiimo, and Todoist each exposed a sign-in form; no account was created and no authenticated planning workflow was available. The accepted screenshots are browser captures of official published UI imagery, saved as JPEG and reopened from disk for inspection. Product imagery may predate the current authenticated UI.

  1. Opened Todoist’s Today guide, observed its dated help content, dismissed the promotional overlay, and opened the guide’s observed Today image URL. Healthy public help access; app task editing blocked by sign-in.
  2. Opened Todoist login, inspected the email/password and provider choices, and stopped. Account wall confirmed; no completion or rescheduling tested.
  3. Opened Motion’s task-manager page, inspected its published task-risk graphic, then Motion’s app. Marketing accessible; app sign-in required. The first marketing media slot reported unavailable playback, so it was not accepted as visual evidence.
  4. Opened Tiimo’s visual-planning page, declined nonessential cookies and scrolled to the day-sequence illustration. Published product visual accessible; Tiimo app sign-in required. Direct image-CDN navigation was blocked by browser policy. No bypass was attempted; the accepted image is the permitted official page in context.

Observable design

Todoist’s published Today example has a white task pane, a pale left navigation column, a short bold sans-serif heading, a quiet task count, circular completion controls and thin horizontal dividers. Three red outlines distinguish selected priorities. Other source rows are deliberately blurred; this is source artwork, not evidence of loading. The useful pattern is the strong reading order and flat row hierarchy. Literal adaptation would risk copying small targets and color-only priority cues.

Tiimo’s page shows a phone plan divided into Anytime and Morning, with a routine containing smaller steps. The routine and steps have icons, completion circles and visible duration. Its surrounding marketing page uses lavender texture, serif display copy, pill navigation and large rounded imagery. Separate the app sequence pattern from that spacious marketing treatment. Celebration artwork is visible but does not prove any interaction or completion behavior in this session.

Tempo direction

Use an agenda desk for Planner: cream canvas, a warm white working sheet, charcoal text, hairline rules and burnt-orange actions. Keep Georgia for page and group headings; use the existing sans-serif for task labels and controls. Proposed spacing is 24–32px between sections and 12–16px within rows; these are Tempo specifications, not measured competitor values. Prefer 10–14px corners on the containing sheet and restrained corners on controls. Avoid a separate tinted card around every task.

Today remains a direct destination with a clear date, one current action and a chronological day rail. Its secondary task/routine panel should not compete with the current item. To-do remains a searchable working list with All, Today, Next 7 days and Done filters. Preserve undated tasks and make their location apparent. Retain in-progress, carryover and future groups, but use text, count and an icon alongside any color.

Keep the established four navigation families—Planner, Daily rhythm, Food & fitness and Brain dump—with Today and Ask Tempo direct. Planner’s related subtabs should share one neutral treatment; the active tab gets an orange underline and label weight. Distinct families should differ in composition, not just tint. On mobile, stack the current action before the remaining agenda; use a horizontally scrollable, labelled subnavigation only if focus remains visible and the selected destination scrolls into view.

Implementation brief and acceptance

  • src/app/workspace/TodayView.tsx and today.module.css: preserve todayModel, ongoing events, current focus, routine-step handlers and calendar failure states; simplify visual containment and reduce repeated emblems.
  • PlanningViews.tsx and planning.module.css: refine the existing grouped checklist rather than rewrite filtering or mutations. Keep completion, reopen, edit and focus as separate actions.
  • WorkspaceNavigation.tsx and navigation.module.css: family/subtab treatment, with direct Today/Ask routes intact. Reuse react-icons/fi; decorative icons stay hidden from assistive technology.
  • Validate 390px and desktop layouts, 200% zoom, keyboard order and visible focus; provide at least 44px activation areas. Test long titles, no tasks, many undated items, overdue tasks, completed/reopened tasks and an active focus session. A timer ending must not imply task completion.
  • Run nearest today-model, today-calendar, and task-date tests, then lint/typecheck; read the relevant installed Next.js guide before editing. These are proposed acceptance checks, not validation performed during research.

Screenshot evidence cannot establish contrast compliance, focus behavior, screen-reader announcements or touch usability. Those require testing Tempo’s rendered implementation.

Implementation status — 2026-10-15

Implemented the To-do agenda sheet in PlanningViews.tsx and planning.module.css: one cream paper surface, quiet status headings, ruled rows, clear checkboxes and restrained secondary focus actions. Existing filtering, counts and callbacks are unchanged. Illustrated goal cards remain intact; shared filter tabs use an orange underline. Today was deliberately left as approved. Nearest calendar/date tests: 17 passed; repository lint and typecheck passed. Browser inventory became unavailable to this subagent, so integrated desktop/mobile visual verification remains assigned to the parent agent.

Study 02 / Planner

Calendar, Goals and Reminders — visual research, 2026-10-15

Read source report ↗

Evidence and attempted flow

All sources below were accessed on 2026-10-15. Evidence level is official marketing/help imagery and documentation. None is an authenticated app audit or public interactive demo. The browser showed sign-in forms at Motion, Tiimo and Todoist; no credentials, purchases, calendar connections or saved mutations were attempted.

  1. Opened Motion’s task-manager feature page, inspected its deadline-risk graphic, and captured the observed official image in the browser. Published evidence accessible. The image is a task-risk illustration, not a live calendar grid.
  2. Read Motion’s current calendar feature description and calendar-purpose documentation. Documentary evidence only. Rescheduling and capacity behavior were not exercised.
  3. Opened Todoist’s reminder guide, observed the September 1, 2026 update marker, and captured its quick-add reminder image. Help visual accessible; reminder creation unavailable behind login.
  4. Inspected Tiimo’s published visual-planning page. Marketing illustration accessible. No calendar event drag, task movement or goals flow was tested.

What the evidence supports

Motion’s image uses a white rounded panel on pale blue, five broadly spaced task rows, outline completion circles and a pink warning strip. Both the warning text and the affected task carry red exclamation marks. A localized problem is legible without repainting the entire list. Motion’s documentation describes time allocation using task deadlines, duration and priority; that is a vendor-described capability, not observed scheduling accuracy.

Todoist’s image shows a task composer and a smaller anchored reminder popover. Date and reminder count appear as chips. Existing reminders are listed above a Date & time / Before task choice, an input, helper copy and an explicit Add reminder action. Gray borders and small outline icons provide hierarchy; red is reserved for the commits. The useful pattern is progressive disclosure with a clear distinction between the task’s time and notification timing. Its narrow floating panel would be cramped on a phone if copied literally. The help text also distinguishes recurring reminders from a one-occurrence date/time reminder; Tempo must describe its own supported semantics explicitly.

There is no captured competitor goal-management flow. Goal recommendations below are original Tempo design proposals grounded in the inspected existing linked-task model, not a claim that another product implements them.

Tempo art direction

Calendar should be the time view of Planner’s agenda sheet. Use a date strip and an open chronological rail with a stable time column; reserve a small blue marker for external calendar events, orange for the selected day/action, and neutral surfaces for most content. A compact connection status belongs near the calendar heading or in a collapsible aside. Avoid a large integration card taking equal weight with the schedule. Do not imply drag rescheduling when existing handlers open an editor or Ask Tempo.

Goals should show a hierarchy of outcome, next step and linked-step progress. Prefer a restrained editorial list or two-column outcome grid with one subtle journey visual, rather than repeating the same large landscape on every card. Progress means linked tasks completed, not verified achievement. Keep Mark achieved explicit and keep linked tasks when removing a goal.

Reminders should be a chronological delivery list: date/time column, reminder sentence, readable state and Change with Tempo. Put future and delivered items in clearly labelled sections; preserve sending-state restrictions. Use a bell icon plus text for delivery state, not badge color alone. Creation can continue through Ask Tempo with an explicit return path. This surface remains related to Calendar and Goals through typography and toolbar rhythm while keeping its own row structure.

Implementation brief and acceptance

  • CalendarView.tsx: reshape presentation only; preserve useCalendarEvents, timezone/date navigation, reconnect, loading/error/truncated/disconnected and preview labels. Keep external edit limitations and confirmations visible when relevant.
  • PlanningViews.tsx / planning.module.css: revise goal layout around linked progress and next action; retain existing achieve, add-step and remove callbacks.
  • SavedContextViews.tsx: scope reminder-specific layout by section or a small dedicated component; avoid changing memory presentation as a side effect. WorkspaceClient.tsx already owns confirmation and Ask Tempo handoff.
  • Use 44px targets, labelled date controls, visible keyboard focus and readable 16px primary rows. At 390px, connection details move below the schedule; date/time labels wrap without overlapping actions. Popovers become an accessible dialog or inline panel if later added.
  • Verify all-day events, overlapping events, DST/timezone dates, very long labels, failed/revoked calendar access, no goals, zero-step goals, completed goals, pending/sending/delivered reminders and search with no results. Confirm destructive buttons remain gated and a stale fetch is not labelled current.
  • Run nearest calendar-event, connection, Today calendar and task-date tests plus lint/typecheck after implementation. No code or app-state changes were made during this study; these tests have not been run for this report.

Screenshots cannot prove accessible contrast, notification delivery, scheduling quality or database idempotency. These remain implementation checks.

Implementation status — 2026-10-15

Implemented CalendarView.tsx with scoped calendar.module.css: seven-day selector, selected-date heading, aligned chronological time/source rows, blue calendar accents and compact connection details below the agenda. Existing events, task/reminder filters, timezone handling, callbacks, sample labels and failure states are preserved. No drag/drop or autoscheduling was added. Goal artwork and actions remain; reminder implementation belongs to the parent team. Calendar/date validation: 4 test files, 17 tests passed; npm run lint and npm run typecheck passed. CUA returned no available browsers in this subagent; rendered desktop/mobile inspection is pending parent verification.

Study 03 / Daily rhythm

Routines, wake and wind-down — visual research, 2026-10-15

Read source report ↗

Evidence and attempted flow

This study used official published product visuals and help, not a hands-on routine or alarm audit. Sources were opened on 2026-10-15. The accepted JPEGs were saved and reopened for visual inspection. No account creation, subscription, hardware pairing, sound playback or alarm scheduling was performed.

  1. Opened Tiimo’s visual-planning page, declined nonessential cookies and inspected the morning routine inside its published day sequence. Public visual available.
  2. Opened Tiimo’s focus page, scrolled to the phone example and captured its current-task timer. Marketing visual available; not an interactive timer.
  3. Opened Tiimo sign-in. App workflow blocked by account requirement; no routine completed. Direct navigation to an image-CDN source was separately blocked by browser security. The accepted captures use the permitted official page context; no bypass was attempted.
  4. Opened Hatch’s sleep-routine article, followed its Wake Up Gently section anchor and scrolled until its sunrise artwork loaded. Supplementary historical marketing visual, article dated August 25, 2023. It shows a named sunrise colorway, not a complete settings interface. Read Hatch’s alarm help for documentary context; hardware/app behavior remains untested.

Observable design and adaptation

Tiimo’s pictured focus screen gives the task name, time range, large circular countdown, numeric remaining time, a small pause control and a checklist. The ring is lavender around a warm illustrated center; the screen itself is predominantly white. A completed step is visibly struck through while the next steps remain readable. Its marketing wrapper has a lavender textured field, large serif headlines and wide two-column spacing. Borrow the task/remaining-time/steps hierarchy, not the photographed phone or marketing whitespace inside Tempo’s working interface. A ring alone would not convey time accessibly; retain numeric time and state text.

Hatch’s accepted visual uses deep textured blue, a large pale sunrise disc with yellow at the horizon, a rounded scene boundary and a pale capsule containing the colorway name. This supports a restrained dawn/night direction, not any claim about current Hatch app controls. The article describes wind-down, sleep and wake as stages; no sleep or clinical outcome is inferred from the visual. Hardware light and alarm capabilities do not transfer to a browser session.

Tempo direction

Daily rhythm should feel like a guided ritual, visibly different from Planner’s agenda sheet. Give the family a horizontal Morning / Evening period choice and a spacious sequence with one highlighted next step. Use the existing cream background, amber/orange for morning and ink/plum for evening. Keep orange on commits and selected controls. Use warm serif routine titles, plain sans-serif step labels, a narrow connecting line and duration labels. Rounded panels can be softer here—about 18–24px—without turning every step into a separate decorative card. Use 24px section gaps and 12–16px between step content; these are proposed Tempo tokens.

The routine summary should show start time, total estimated duration and completed-step count, then a prominent Start next step action. Completed steps remain available behind an unobtrusive expansion, and check/uncheck remains independent from starting a timer. Do not infer completion from elapsed time. Keep editing in the existing routine editor, including conflict/reload behavior.

Wake & wind-down should be a small experience studio: Sunrise / Sunset choice, a wide real light-session preview, duration, one primary Start button and Try 20 seconds. Put sound and maximum glow under a clear disclosure. The preview must reflect the selected mode and values. During a session, show remaining time, progress and End session in stable positions; do not hide the exit behind an icon that becomes unreadable over changing light. Preserve the product’s truthful boundary: this is a session for now, not a scheduled device alarm. Audio starts only with an explicit start action; screen colors do not control physical brightness or wake sleeping hardware.

Implementation brief and acceptance

  • WorkspaceClient.tsx currently owns routineCard, routine completion and start-step callbacks. Prefer a cohesive RoutinesView.tsx plus scoped styles if layout complexity warrants extraction; keep mutations in the existing owner.
  • RoutineEditor.tsx / routine-editor.module.css: retain step order, duration, period, time and stale-version handling. Add no unsupported recurrence promises.
  • WakeExperience.tsx, wake.module.css and wake-session.ts: refine the existing two-mode selection and preview; preserve audio cleanup, wake-lock fallback, dialog focus loop, Escape/end behavior and user-controlled fullscreen.
  • Mobile: one column, 44px minimum controls, no hover-only actions, long step titles wrapping beside durations, and generous separation between complete and play. Respect reduced motion; provide time/state text and a nonmoving preview. Test contrast through the light range and focus against both modes.
  • Acceptance scenarios: empty and long routines; completed-day reset; active/paused focus; switching family mid-session; preview and normal duration; sound failure; unavailable wake lock; fullscreen failure; Escape; tab focus containment; reduced motion; 390px width and 200% zoom. Confirm stopping leaves unfinished work unfinished.
  • Run nearest RoutineEditor, today-model and wake-session tests plus lint/typecheck after implementation. This research changed only documentation and evidence files; no runtime validation is claimed.

Implemented first pass

WorkspaceClient.tsx now groups routines into Morning and Evening columns on a warm ritual surface. Each routine shows time, duration, daily progress, a current/next-step action, and its full interactive checklist. Existing edit, check, focus and undo callbacks remain. rhythm-brain.module.css supplies the scoped treatment. Wake uses the same family wrapper; its session implementation, capability warnings, audio, wake lock, fullscreen and exit controls are unchanged. Validation: 33 nearest tests passed across RoutineEditor, today-model, wake-session, SavedContextViews and ChatMessage; lint and typecheck passed. Browser review pending.

Study 04 / Food & fitness

Nutrition Tracker: evidence and implementation brief

Read source report ↗

Research date: 2026-10-15. Scope: Tempo's current Food log, renamed Nutrition Tracker. This is visual research with one anonymous public planner interaction, not a completed authenticated diary audit.

Sources and steps actually attempted

  1. Opened the user-supplied Eat This Much comparison, Paprika section. Editorial/vendor comparison; readable. Used it to identify organization patterns, not to validate competitors' prices, accuracy, or popularity. Its Paprika table and body describe different pricing models, so those claims are excluded.
  2. Opened MyFitnessPal, followed “Log in,” dismissed the privacy notice, and reached Member Login. Actual web entry flow; blocked at authentication. No credentials entered, account created, CAPTCHA action, or diary reached. Browser 2 / tab 2 was retained for optional user sign-in.
  3. Opened Eat This Much, retained its public default settings, clicked Generate, and observed a completed 1,806-calorie sample plan. Interactive anonymous public demo; successful. This number is the site's sample output, not a Tempo target or recommendation. No account persistence was tested.
  4. Inspected the user's provided mobile diary screenshot. User-supplied visual reference, not evidence of a product interaction in this run.
  5. After the user could not sign in, inspected MyFitnessPal's official Today guide, including its meal and macro illustrations. Official help screens, not authenticated hands-on testing. They confirm a diary-first layout, date selection, calorie/macro summaries and per-meal Log actions. No claim of a live diary interaction is made.

Observations

The supplied reference is the strongest direction: a pale blue field, compact white rounded cards, a seven-day strip, a wide calorie bar, three macro bars, and separate Breakfast/Lunch/Dinner cards. A strong number leads each summary; the meal name and Log action share a row. Its close spacing keeps the diary usable without a promotional hero. Small secondary type and faint outlines are visible accessibility risks; Tempo should increase these rather than reproduce their weakness.

The MyFitnessPal login uses a strong blue brand panel and a narrow white form with rounded light-gray fields. It confirms an account boundary only; it supplies no evidence about the current web diary. The official homepage advertises photo, voice, and barcode capture, but those were not exercised and should not appear as working Tempo capabilities.

Eat This Much's generated plan has a clear hierarchy: day total, meal heading, meal calories, photo, food title, portion. Regeneration sits beside the day and each meal. The dense, left-aligned rows make portions easy to scan. Its white background, dark text, blue links and orange registration rail differ from the user's desired calm blue cards. The registration rail takes substantial width; Tempo can use that space for the diary itself.

Tempo brief

Own src/app/workspace/NutritionView.tsx and scoped additions to food-fitness.module.css. Coordinate naming with the owner of WorkspaceNavigation.tsx and WorkspaceClient.tsx; keep the internal nutrition section identifier stable. Reuse FoodSearch, FoodEntryEditor, NutritionTargetEditor, and nutrition-model.ts contracts.

Build a pale blue nutrition panel with a compact date toolbar and seven-day selection strip. Lead with a white calorie card: logged value, optional user target, and remaining/over-target text only when both values are known. Use a horizontal bar instead of the large decorative circle. Unknown values remain “—”; incomplete totals remain explicitly partial. Without a target, show the logged value and “No daily target,” never fabricate a denominator.

Follow with three equal macro cards using teal, purple, and gold; show fiber as a smaller supporting metric. Keep labels and gram values visible so color is supplementary. Use approximately 18–24px card corners, restrained borders, 16px body type, and 12–16px inner gaps as proposed Tempo choices, not measured competitor specifications.

The diary is the primary work area: four meal groups, counts and known calorie subtotals, clear Add food action, compact portion/nutrient rows. Preserve favorite, copy, edit, delete, search/scan, manual entry and targets. Give empty meals a calm one-line invitation. Keep source attribution and selected-day loading/truncation warnings visible.

Acceptance and validation

  • Desktop shows date, summary, macros and the first diary groups without a large empty hero; mobile stacks cleanly at 390px without clipping actions.
  • Date changes select the same diary data as the existing date input. No plan becomes consumed food automatically.
  • Optional targets, zero values, missing values, partial totals, portions/units and Open Food Facts attribution survive the redesign.
  • Keyboard focus is visible; date buttons expose selection; icon actions retain labels; bars include text equivalents.
  • Run nutrition-model.test.ts, repository lint and typecheck, then browser-check desktop/mobile, empty/partial/target states and editor opening. Read the relevant local Next.js guide before edits. These are required follow-up checks; no application tests were run for this documentation-only research.

Implementation pass, 2026-10-15

Implemented in NutritionView.tsx and new scoped nutrition.module.css: seven-day selection with week navigation, compact calorie bar, teal/purple/gold macro cards, and four rounded meal cards. All existing editors, search/scan, favorite/copy/edit/delete and source attribution remain connected. Targets stay optional; zero targets never become invalid progress denominators, and partial intake cannot produce a misleading remaining value. Added meaningful rendering assertions for complete, partial, absent and zero targets. Nearest three food/fitness test files passed (19 tests); npm run lint and npm run typecheck passed. Parent agent owns final browser QA, global naming, and final full validation.

Study 05 / Food & fitness

Meals, Recipes, Groceries and Pantry: evidence and brief

Read source report ↗

Research date: 2026-10-15. Goal: related food pages with distinct working layouts. This report uses an anonymous public planner and official Paprika help screenshots; Paprika itself was not installed or operated.

Sources and steps actually attempted

  1. Read the supplied vendor comparison. Editorial evidence; readable. Its Paprika section motivated checking the primary guide. Its inconsistent pricing descriptions are not carried forward.
  2. Used Eat This Much's public generator: clicked Generate with defaults and observed completed Breakfast, Lunch and Dinner rows with food names, photos, portions and per-meal calories. Interactive public demo; successful. Did not sign up, save a plan, or test shopping/pantry.
  3. Opened Paprika's official site, expanded User Guides, selected Windows, then used the Meals and Groceries contents links. Official help evidence; successful reading. Captured and inspected those page sections. The guide explicitly identifies Paprika 3 and offers a separate Paprika 4 beta guide; the screenshots are historical product illustrations, not live current-version screenshots.
  4. Read the same guide's recipe, grocery and pantry explanations. Documented behavior only. No grocery item, recipe or pantry inventory was changed.

Findings grounded in the evidence

The Paprika meal guide shows Day/Week/Month controls, date navigation, colored meal-type text, shallow date bands and a recipe sidebar with small thumbnails. Planning is the central surface; recipes are a supporting source. Its dense rows are more efficient than giving every empty meal a large image. On a small viewport, the narrow recipe rail and tiny labels would need a different arrangement.

The recipe guide documents category and field search, list/photo-grid views, recipe metadata, scaling, and separate ingredients/directions. That separation suggests a browsable library with detail disclosure, rather than a page filled with simultaneously expanded recipe text. The captured meal view is visual evidence; recipe-detail behavior remains help documentation.

The grocery guide shows aisle headers, checkboxes, ingredient quantities and source-recipe subtitles. It documents reviewable ingredient selection before adding a recipe, and consolidation of similar ingredients. The pantry guide documents quantity, stock status and dates, with pantry ingredients unchecked during grocery addition. These are useful interaction patterns, but do not establish compatible unit conversion or automatic stock accounting in Tempo.

Eat This Much reinforces meal-group hierarchy and visible portions. Its completed public plan is easy to read but spends a large right column on registration. Tempo can replace that marketing rail with the user's selected-day details. The supplied diary reference supplies the shared pale blue / white rounded-card language; Paprika supplies structure, not its desktop-era gray-table styling.

Tempo implementation brief

Primary ownership: src/app/workspace/FoodViews.tsx, scoped food styles in food-fitness.module.css, and only necessary rules in workspace.module.css. Preserve MealPhoto, demo-recipes.ts, existing editors/callbacks, and src/server/domain/meal-groceries.ts semantics.

Meals: keep a compact week strip, date input and Plan a meal action. Use a broad selected-day planner with meal groups as rows/cards and small dish thumbnails, plus a narrower “From your recipes” area when space permits. Clearly label preparing servings and ingredient basis. Put shopping inclusion and recipe swap behind an intentional details region, retaining access. Empty meals need a short Add meal affordance, not a large repeated decorative illustration. State that plans are separate from eaten food.

Recipes: use a white photo-card library inside the blue family surface, searchable by title locally if implemented. Cards lead with photo, title, preparation time and servings. Save and Plan actions stay distinct. Ingredients and directions open in a readable details area. Keep saved recipes above a clearly marked recipe-ideas shelf; sample ideas must not look saved until their existing save succeeds.

Groceries/Pantry: use a task-focused shopping list with an adjacent stock panel on desktop, stacked on mobile. Keep unchecked shopping items first; label purchased items “not added to pantry.” Retain quantities, units and source-meal links. Group only by data the app actually knows; do not fabricate aisles, expiration dates or inventory amounts. Unknown pantry amounts remain unknown and cannot reduce shopping calculations.

Acceptance and validation

  • Each page has a distinct primary layout while sharing blue surfaces, rounded white cards and consistent type.
  • Plans, recipes, purchases, pantry and consumed food remain distinct records and actions.
  • Servings scale through existing behavior; manual groceries remain separate; derived groceries edit their source meal.
  • Buttons, disclosure controls, checkboxes and photo alt text remain accessible at desktop/mobile widths.
