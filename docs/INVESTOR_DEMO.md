## A five-minute walkthrough

1. **Today:** start the project paragraph’s focus timer, pause it, then return to the day. Show the routine checklist and check one step. Use Undo to restore it.
2. **Goals → To-do:** open the design-project steps. Its completed outline, scheduled paragraph, and future draft handoff show how a longer-term goal becomes small actions.
3. **Fitness:** select full-body, push/pull/legs, or the at-home template. Open a session, choose Plan this session, set a date and save. It appears in To-do; completed movement minutes do not change. Sessions are individual plans, not automatically recurring programs.
4. **Recipes → Meal plan:** open a recipe’s quantities and preparation steps, then Plan a meal. Saving opens the chosen meal date. The seven-day menu has breakfast, lunch and dinner; snacks are deliberately optional. Grocery checkboxes and food logging remain separate actions.
5. **Brain dump:** show the Sunday-reset note, project idea and saved preferences. Edit a note, or send it to To-do with the existing control.
6. **Ask Tempo:** introduce this explicitly as a scripted example illustrating the intended conversation. It does not call a model or send texts. For live assistant evidence, use a separately verified authenticated demo account.
7. **Sunrise & sunset:** run the foreground visual session. Describe physical brightness, reliable scheduled alarms and waking a device as unverified hardware work.

## Accuracy and boundaries

Meal images are AI-illustrated serving suggestions, not photographs of a measured portion. They appear only when the saved dish name and ingredients match the supplied recipe. A changed recipe does not silently keep an inaccurate picture. Nutrition values are illustrative manual estimates, not calculated from the recipe images. Meal planning does not log food eaten. Changing planned servings preserves and labels the original recipe quantities instead of pretending to scale arbitrary free text.

Workout programs are general editable examples, not personalized clinical or trainer-authored prescriptions. The home circuit is a starting point, not a complete fitness program. Program cards cite the [CDC’s general adult activity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html); that source supports the general activity context, not these specific exercise selections. Chicken preparation uses the [USDA’s 165°F / 74°C minimum poultry temperature](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart). Recipes are original examples, not externally tested recipes.

The existing workspace actions, authentication, consent, durable storage and provider boundaries are unchanged. Live account persistence, AI, SMS, Google Calendar, barcode scanning, physical hardware and multi-user scale were not validated by this preview review. Do not present the reviewer’s demo score as commercial readiness.
